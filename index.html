The framework

Speech is more
than a transcript.

Spoken role-play depends on prosody, emotion, character embodiment, and contextual fit. A fluent transcript alone cannot tell us whether a performance works.

DRAME-EvalBench pairs bilingual human judgments with two complementary evaluation protocols and contrastive mismatches. DRAME-Eval, a fine-tuned audio-language evaluator, learns to estimate these judgments from speech and textual context.

Archetype evaluation

Start with a role. Listen for a fit.

A top-down protocol uses role and scene prompts to assess generated speech: audio quality, human likeness, and appropriateness, with a separate content-pass check.

Realism evaluation

Start with a performance. Examine the details.

A bottom-up protocol anchors context in human recordings and assesses ten delivery and role-fit dimensions. Mismatched roles, scenes, and delivery expose different evaluator failure modes.

Benchmark alignment does not carry over to authentic acting.

On authentic human speech, within-speaker correlation is only +0.029, compared with matched human single-rater agreement of +0.34. The result identifies a synthetic-to-authentic evaluation gap and motivates better evaluation of differences among human performances.

Main results use masking for unavailable labels. Pooled bilingual correlations can reflect between-language effects; see the paper for per-language results, uncertainty estimates, and controls. The authentic-speech pool is partitioned into 1,080 adaptation records and 1,084 clean test records. The entire pool is not a held-out test set.
